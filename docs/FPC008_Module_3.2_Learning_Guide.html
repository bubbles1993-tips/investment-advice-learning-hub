01Reliability & validity

Consistent evidence can still answer the wrong question.

Reliability

Would another competent person using equivalent inputs and the same method obtain a similar result? Reproducible calculations, dated records and stable definitions support reliability.

Validity

Does the measure address the actual decision? A perfectly reproducible share-price chart may be invalid evidence for a claim about total return or future withdrawal access.

Research integrity involves both. Start with the decision the client needs to make, identify what evidence could change that decision, and test whether the source actually establishes the claim. A glossy report, a precise decimal or an independent author is not sufficient by itself.

ClaimUseful evidenceWeak substitute
Money can be withdrawn by a deadlineCurrent redemption terms, dealing timetable, settlement and relevant restrictionsA daily unit price
The investment matches a stated exclusionDefined screen, thresholds, coverage and holdings evidenceA green-themed name
A manager added valueComparable net returns, a suitable benchmark and attribution over relevant periodsA single strong calendar year
The client can fund a recurring contributionReconciled income, expenditure and contingenciesAn unexplained annual surplus estimate
Original check: repeatable but invalid

A researcher accurately computes a product’s price rise every month, then uses it to rank total returns against an index that reinvests distributions. The arithmetic may be reliable. The comparison is invalid because the return definitions do not match.

A score supports judgement; it cannot approve reliance.

The course uses a source-quality rubric. Treat it as a prompt to investigate. A high total cannot compensate for missing withdrawal terms when the decision depends on access to cash.

02Client evidence as research

Collect what the question needs, then resolve the gaps.

Frame an advice question with a goal, action under consideration, deadline and relevant constraints. This controls the scope of fact-finding and prevents a product list from becoming the starting point. For example: “Can Jules fund a study break in three years while keeping the agreed emergency reserve and mortgage commitments?”

Quantitative inputs

Cash-flow amounts and variability, dated asset and debt balances, ownership, access, cost bases, product terms and protection needs relevant to the question.

Qualitative inputs

Goal priorities, values, flexibility, risk willingness, experiences, decision authority, family responsibilities and which outcomes would be unacceptable.

Use neutral questions and reflect the client’s meaning back. Check reported income against relevant records, remove transfers from spending analysis, reconcile ownership and dates, and investigate conflicts. Keep original estimates distinct from verified figures rather than silently replacing them.

Evidence statusHow to use it
Verified for this purposeRecord the source, date, definition and why it supports the claim.
Plausible but incompleteIdentify the missing check, who will complete it and how a wrong assumption could change the conclusion.
Conflicting or unsupportedInvestigate before relying on a material fact. Acknowledgement of uncertainty does not make unsuitable advice appropriate.

Translate values into measurable settings: the activity to exclude, revenue threshold, look-through treatment, exceptions, data coverage and review approach. Then test the effect on cost, diversification, liquidity and benchmark differences. A non-negotiable value should not quietly disappear from a compromise option.

Original conflict: a bonus funds the plan

The client expects a $20,000 bonus, but the current contract makes it discretionary and past payments vary. Keep guaranteed salary and uncertain bonus separate. Model the goal without the bonus, seek relevant evidence and decide whether the proposed commitment remains affordable. Do not call an optimistic estimate verified.

Collect proportionately, explain the purpose, restrict access and correct errors. Avoid putting real client identifiers or documents into this study guide’s notes; use fictional cases. More collected data does not necessarily mean better research.

Course coverage: §2.2, PDF pp. 5–8.

03A defensible workflow

Choose a strategy before choosing its wrapper.

StagePractical output
1. FrameThe question, scope, goal, date and decisive constraints.
2. ValidateA reconciled evidence set with assumptions and unresolved items clearly labelled.
3. Compare strategiesFeasible options, including the status quo where relevant, with costs, risks and trade-offs.
4. Research implementationA documented product universe and consistent checks of mandate, fees, liquidity, tax structure and operational risks.
5. ChallengeDownside cases, contrary evidence, benchmark fit, conflicts and values alignment.
6. Decide and explainA reason linked to the client, reasons alternatives were rejected, assumptions and understanding checks.
7. Implement and reviewRequired authority, dependencies, completion evidence and owned review triggers.

A platform menu is an implementation constraint, not evidence that every listed product is suitable or that all relevant alternatives were considered. Record the search boundaries and material influences on selection. Familiarity and prior research effort should not make the status quo the automatic winner.

Original comparison: a future lump sum

Consider three feasible paths to a planned payment: retain accessible savings, stage investment of only the flexible portion, or reduce the goal’s funding gap through additional saving. Compare the same deadline, cash needs, downside assumptions and costs. Only after settling the strategy should specific products be selected to implement it.

TMD is research, not a personal suitability verdict.

ASIC expects advisers to consider relevant TMD information. Personal advice has an exemption from the DDO reasonable-steps obligation, while reporting and record requirements remain. General advice and execution-only services differ. ASIC INFO 264 explains the distinction.

Complete the required advice, consent and implementation checks before acting. Sequence actions around actual dependencies: cash must be available before a payment; a replacement arrangement must be established before an action that depends on it. A generic sequence is not a substitute for checking the file.

Course coverage: §2.3, PDF pp. 8–10; §2.7–§2.8, pp. 18–24. ASIC clarification checked 10 November 2026.

04Audit & reconcile sources

Independence and authority are different strengths.

Current issuer documents are authoritative evidence of product terms, but they are not independent analysis. An external research report may provide useful challenge while being stale or commercially conflicted. A factsheet and a website repeating the same issuer data are not two independent checks.

SourceUse it forCheck before relying
PDS, constitution/deed and noticesMandate, powers, fees, withdrawals, valuation and changesVersion, effective date, supplements and any inconsistent wording.
TMDTarget class, distribution and review informationCurrent version and the applicable conduct obligations.
Factsheets and holdingsRecent performance, exposures and portfolio characteristicsDefinitions, completeness, date, currency and fee basis.
External research or dataCross-checking, methodology and critical perspectiveFunding, conflicts, independence of the underlying data and update cycle.
Assurance and operational reportsEvidence about the controls examinedPeriod, scope, qualifications and what the report did not test.

Audit seven dimensions: independence, method transparency, replicability, benchmark fit, timeliness, consistency and completeness. The course’s two-source approach is a research discipline for material claims, not a universal legal safe harbour. Two agreeing summaries can still share the same error.

Source-audit practice

Select the evidence status for each dimension. This original tool identifies gaps without producing an approval score. It uses fictional evidence and does not save selections.

Original reconciliation: a withdrawal delay

A current product notice states monthly withdrawals with 20 business days’ notice. An older external report says weekly withdrawals. Check effective dates and whether the report refers to a different class or product. Obtain confirmation of the current terms and any suspension powers. Record the relied-on terms and reassess the client’s deadline; do not average the two descriptions.

A reconciliation note should preserve the conflicting claims, source dates, investigation, resolution, chosen basis and impact on the decision. If a material conflict remains unresolved, obtain stronger evidence or reconsider reliance. A verbal assurance should be recorded and, where material, corroborated.

Course coverage: §2.4–§2.5, PDF pp. 10–15.

05Manager & sustainability checks

Follow the claim through to what is actually done.

AreaEvidence to seekWarning sign
PeopleTeam responsibilities, turnover, incentives and successionA star-person story without depth or continuity.
Philosophy and processResearch method, portfolio construction and sell disciplineAn investment “edge” that cannot be explained or tested.
PerformanceNet outcomes, attribution, style consistency and drawdownsReturns credited to skill with no account of factor exposure.
RiskPosition, sector, liquidity and derivative controls; breach responsesLimits on paper without monitoring or escalation evidence.
OperationsCustody, administration, valuation, audits and continuity arrangementsOpaque pricing, unresolved control failures or unclear asset custody.
Fees and capacityFull fee mechanics, trading costs, liquidity and capacity analysisGrowing assets without testing whether the strategy can still trade effectively.

Ask for examples that test the process: a position exited after the thesis changed, a limit breach and its response, a difficult market regime, or evidence supporting a capacity threshold. Specific records carry more weight than assurances that a strategy works in every environment.

For sustainability claims, trace the chain from policy to research and portfolio rules, actual holdings, voting or engagement, and measured outcomes. Define terms, exclusions, thresholds and exceptions. Distinguish a portfolio metric, such as reported carbon intensity, from evidence that the investment caused a real-world reduction.

Original sustainability challenge

A fund calls itself “low carbon”. Establish what is measured, the baseline and comparison, emissions coverage, data gaps, treatment of cash and derivatives, and the reason for changes over time. Lower reported intensity could come from selling a holding or changing estimates rather than an investee reducing emissions. Check the claim that is actually being made.

ASIC’s guidance asks issuers to support sustainability representations and explain screens and limitations clearly. Vague labels and unsupported future claims can mislead. ASIC INFO 271: avoiding greenwashing.

An exclusion and engagement are different approaches.

A fund may exclude an activity, integrate financially material ESG risks, engage with issuers, or pursue impact objectives. Verify which approach is promised and whether it matches the client’s meaning.

Course coverage: §2.6, PDF pp. 15–18; sustainability checks also in §§2.4–2.5.

06Judgement & review

Keep the decision traceable when the facts change.

A concise decision memo should identify the question and constraints, viable options, selected approach and reasons, the main trade-off, and the evidence of client understanding. Cross-reference the source pack and calculations so a reviewer can reconstruct the reasoning without guessing.

TriggerMeasurement and response to define
Liquidity deteriorationWhich withdrawal delay, gate, spread or cash-coverage measure prompts investigation, and by whom.
Manager or process changeWhich personnel, ownership or mandate changes require renewed due diligence.
Style or active-risk driftBenchmark, window, metric and persistence required for a review; tracking error is not a maximum return gap.
Client changeIncome, liabilities, goals, health or family changes requiring refreshed facts and modelling.
Rules or product termsWho checks effective dates and the consequences for the strategy.

Every trigger needs an owner, data source, observation frequency, action and follow-up date. A review is an investigation with a decision, not necessarily an automatic trade. Retain the old assumptions and the reason for changes so the record tells a coherent story over time.

Original final challenge: all seven audit fields are supported

The research pack is current, reproducible and well corroborated, but the proposed investment has a lock-up beyond the client’s payment deadline. Good source quality has revealed an unsuitable fit. The correct next step is to change the strategy or product, not to use the audit result as approval.

Challenge confirmation bias by recording credible contrary evidence; challenge sunk cost by comparing choices afresh; challenge present bias by considering longer-term goal sufficiency; challenge overconfidence with ranges and plausible adverse cases. Disclosure alone does not cure a conflict or a defective recommendation.

Your final teach-back

Explain the difference between “the source is credible”, “the claim is supported” and “the recommendation fits this client”. Give a different piece of evidence for each. Together, Topics 1–3 connect discovery, constraints and research into a defensible decision.

Course coverage: §2.7–§2.8, PDF pp. 18–24.

Designing within client constraints

Turn risk, time, access, tax and personal boundaries into a portfolio policy that can work in the real world.

01Map the constraints

A good portfolio must be possible to live with.

Topic 1 established the client’s goals and resources. Topic 2 asks which choices remain feasible once the client’s boundaries are recognised. An attractive expected return is irrelevant if the money cannot be accessed when it is needed, or the client cannot withstand the loss.

Objective versus constraint

An objective describes the result sought: for example, funding a home deposit by a date. A constraint restricts how the result can be pursued: the money must be accessible and a large loss would make the deposit unaffordable.

Willingness versus capacity

Willingness concerns emotional comfort with uncertainty. Capacity concerns the financial consequences of loss. High enthusiasm does not repair a short horizon or an inadequate cash reserve.

BoundaryEvidence to establishPortfolio consequence
RiskLoss capacity, willingness and goal flexibilityChoose a risk level the plan and the person can support.
TimeEach goal’s amount, date and prioritySeparate near-term spending from long-term capital.
LiquidityWithdrawals, emergencies and product exit termsKeep accessible funding and test stressed access.
Tax and legalOwner, structure, tax records and restrictionsCompare feasible after-tax outcomes and lawful implementation.
Individual circumstancesValues, employer shares, family commitments and bequestsDefine screens, concentration limits and special instructions.

Distinguish a hard restriction from a negotiable preference. A legal prohibition cannot be traded away for a higher forecast return. A flexible holiday date may be negotiable. A values preference deserves a precise conversation; it is not automatically a behavioural error.

Original example: a confident investor with a short deadline

Mira wants to use $65,000 for a deposit in 14 months and says she is comfortable with sharemarket volatility. Ask what happens if the balance falls before settlement. Her willingness may be high while the deposit’s loss capacity is low. Discuss the deadline, required amount, accessible funding and alternatives before selecting an allocation.

02Horizon & withdrawals

One client can have several investment clocks.

The relevant horizon belongs to a goal, not simply to the client’s age. A household may need school fees next year, retirement income for decades and a bequest much later. Map when the capital will be spent and how flexible those dates are.

Accumulating

Contributions and future earnings may provide room to absorb volatility. Still check job security, debt, emergency needs and whether income depends on the same industry as investments.

Drawing down

Withdrawals can force the sale of more units after losses. Poor early returns can damage sustainability even if a later recovery occurs. A longer retirement horizon does not remove this sequencing risk.

A cash ladder assigns resources to dated spending needs. Longer-term assets can then be considered for later goals. A glide path changes the mix over time, but it must reflect changing circumstances; an age-based default is not a substitute for reassessment. Cash reduces some sale pressure but carries inflation and opportunity costs.

Coverage in months = accessible reserve ÷ monthly net portfolio withdrawalUse the amount the portfolio must actually fund after other reliable cash income. Keep emergency reserves and known one-off bills separate to avoid counting the same dollars twice.

Cash runway lab

Fictional AUD amounts. The calculation first sets aside a one-off bill and a separate emergency reserve. Remaining cash funds constant monthly withdrawals. It excludes interest, inflation, taxes and changing income. No universal buffer target is implied.

03Liquidity under pressure

A market value is not a promise of immediate cash.

Liquidity has several layers: whether an asset can be sold, at what price, how much can be sold without large market impact, and when settled cash reaches the client. Normal-market trading and stressed-market access can differ sharply.

Holding or mechanismWhat to investigate
Listed securityOrder-book depth, spread, normal and stressed trading volume, position size, trading restrictions and settlement.
Managed fundRedemption notice, dealing frequency, suspension or gate powers, asset liquidity, spreads and settlement timing.
Property or private assetsValuation frequency, sale process, lock-ups, capital calls and the possibility of a steep discount.
Term deposit or preserved superContractual early-access terms or legal access conditions; a displayed balance alone does not establish availability.
Estimated trading days = position ÷ (average daily traded amount × participation rate)Use shares with shares, or dollars with dollars. Participation is the fraction of market volume the trade assumes it can use. This is an estimate, not an execution or price guarantee.

Days-to-liquidate lab

Dollar amounts are used consistently. Participation is entered as a percentage. The estimate ignores market closures, price moves and settlement. Halve the daily volume to explore a liquidity shock.

The starting position takes an estimated six trading days, rising to twelve at half the volume. A pooled fund needs a separate assessment of its underlying liquidity and redemption terms. Holding an ETF or fund does not make every underlying asset instantly liquid.

Access has a price.

Larger reserves reduce reliance on forced sales but may reduce expected growth. Very small reserves can leave the plan dependent on selling during a downturn. Size the reserve from cash needs and stress tests, then specify how and when it will be replenished.

04Tax, rules & ownership

Optimise the whole outcome, not one tax number.

Separate asset allocation (what is owned) from asset location (the account or ownership structure holding it). Tax treatment, access, administration, control and estate consequences can differ between personal ownership, companies, trusts and super. A structure with a lower tax rate may be unsuitable if the money is unavailable when needed.

Research questionWhy it changes the decision
Who owns each asset and what is its cost base?The same market value can imply different realised gains and tax consequences.
Is the return income, realised gain or unrealised growth?Taxable income, cash receipts and economic return need separate treatment.
What would a staged sale preserve and expose?It may spread realisations but leaves concentration risk in place for longer.
What terms or rules restrict action?Preservation, caps, trust powers, mandates, trading windows or disclosure requirements may constrain the choice.

Franking credits are tax attributes, not additional dividend cash. Managed-fund tax components require the relevant tax statement; a distribution yield does not identify them. Compare the benefits of a change with transaction costs, spreads, tax and continuing risk.

Do not turn a tax shortcut into a rule.

A capital loss does not automatically create an immediate cash refund. In Australia it is generally applied against capital gains, with unused net capital losses carried forward; eligibility and ordering matter. ATO: using capital losses explains the treatment. Check the current rules and the actual owner’s position before modelling a sale.

Use a product’s PDS, governing terms and TMD as evidence. The TMD describes a class of consumers; it does not decide suitability for a particular client. ASIC explains that personal advice is exempt from the DDO reasonable-steps obligation, while reporting and record obligations remain. ASIC’s personal-advice DDO guidance.

Original trade-off: retain a large holding to avoid tax?

Suppose Rowan holds a substantial legacy position with an embedded gain. Compare the status quo, a full sale and a staged reduction. Estimate tax and costs for each, then show remaining single-company exposure and cash availability. Minimising tax alone does not establish the best overall option. Record why the selected balance fits the goal and what would trigger an earlier review.

05Portfolio theory in context

A constraint changes the choices available.

Modern Portfolio Theory evaluates assets together using expected returns, variances and correlations. Diversification depends on how returns co-move, rather than just the number of holdings. A hundred securities sharing one risk driver may still leave a concentrated portfolio.

Expected portfolio return = Σ weight × expected asset returnOriginal example: 50% at an assumed 7%, 35% at 4%, and 15% at 2% gives 5.20% before costs and tax. The inputs are hypothetical expectations, not forecasts.

For fixed assumptions, adding a binding constraint removes feasible portfolios: the constrained optimum cannot improve on the unrestricted optimum. A non-binding limit may make no difference. This mathematical statement does not mean an ethical screen always lowers realised returns, or that an unrestricted theoretical portfolio is suitable.

Systematic risk

Market-related risk remains even in a well-diversified portfolio. CAPM links expected return to beta, the sensitivity to market returns.

Idiosyncratic risk

Company-specific exposure may be reduced through diversification. A concentrated holding can add risk that CAPM does not reward with extra expected return.

CAPM expected return = risk-free rate + β × (expected market return − risk-free rate)With hypothetical rates of 3% and 8% and beta 0.8: 3% + 0.8 × 5% = 7%. This is a model estimate, not a promised return.
Beta is not total volatility.

Beta measures sensitivity to a chosen market, estimated over a period. A low-beta security can still have substantial company-specific risk. Correlations, betas and expected returns can change, especially during stress.

Values screens, retained employer shares and legal limits can change factor exposure, liquidity and benchmark fit. Rebuild diversification within the permissible universe and compare the resulting mix with a suitable reference. An index hedge may reduce broad market exposure without removing the retained company’s specific risk.

06From evidence to an IPS

Make every limit explainable and reviewable.

An Investment Policy Statement records how the portfolio is intended to serve the client. It connects objectives and constraints to permitted assets, allocation ranges, cash policy, rebalancing and review responsibilities. It supports the advice record; it does not replace required advice documents or implementation authority.

DecisionA useful record includes
ConcentrationWhich holdings are measured together, the denominator, limit, transition plan and exception process.
ValuesSpecific excluded activities, thresholds, data coverage, exceptions and monitoring.
CashPurpose of the reserve, eligible assets, target, minimum, replenishment method and responsible reviewer.
RebalancingTarget weights, bands stated in percentage points, timing, tax/cost checks and who may act.
ReviewMetric, data source, threshold, observation window, action, owner and deadline.

For example, a 40% target with a ±4 percentage-point band means 36–44%, not 38.4–41.6%. A review trigger should prompt the agreed investigation; it need not force an automatic sale regardless of costs, taxes or changing facts.

Original practice: write one complete trigger

Fictional policy: the adviser checks accessible cash against the next year’s agreed net withdrawals at each quarterly review. If coverage falls below the agreed minimum, the adviser updates cash-flow assumptions and discusses replenishment within ten business days. Record the chosen minimum and why it fits this client. The example supplies a structure, not a recommended buffer size.

Before finalising, compare feasible alternatives, show downside cases, explain the main sacrifice and check the client’s understanding. The course connects this work to Code Standards 2, 5 and 6: integrity and best interests, appropriate and understood advice, and broader long-term effects. Read the Code itself for the exact wording.

Teach-back prompt

Explain how a longer horizon can coexist with an urgent cash need. Then name one policy setting, one supporting source and one event that would make you revisit it.
